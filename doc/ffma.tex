\chapter{Fused Multiply-Add Unit (ffma)}
\label{ch:ffma}

\section{Introduction}

\sig{ffma} computes $\mathtt{op\_a}\times\mathtt{op\_b}+\mathtt{op\_c}$ with a
single rounding, one operation per clock cycle. Three \sig{unpack} modules split the
operands. The \sig{ffma\_core} module multiplies $a$ and $b$ exactly, aligns the
product and $c$ as magnitudes, shifting the smaller one right with a sticky bit, adds
them in two's complement and normalizes the sum, as \sig{fmac\_core} does with its
accumulator. \sig{pack} rounds the sum in the mode of \sig{rnd\_mode}, as
Figure~\ref{fig:ffma} shows.

\section{Features}

\begin{itemize}
\item A fused multiply-add, correctly rounded, one operation per clock cycle.
\item A latency of 6 clock cycles.
\item The rounding mode is \sig{rnd\_mode} with the operands.
\item Subnormal operands and results.
\item NaN and infinity handling, selected by \sig{SPECIAL\_CASES}.
\end{itemize}

\section{Instantiation}

The example below instantiates \sig{ffma} for binary32.
Compile it with the files of Table~\ref{tab:files}.

\begin{vcode}
ffma #(
   .DATA_W       (32),
   .EXP_W        (8),
   .SPECIAL_CASES(1)
) ffma0 (
   .clk     (clk),
   .rst     (rst),
   .valid_i (valid_i),
   .valid_o (valid_o),
   .rnd_mode(rnd_mode),
   .op_a    (op_a),
   .op_b    (op_b),
   .op_c    (op_c),
   .res     (res)
);
\end{vcode}

\section{Operation}

Raise \sig{valid\_i} with each set of operands; \sig{valid\_o} is high with the
result 6 clock cycles later. The special results follow IEEE~754: NaN if any operand
is NaN, for infinity times zero, or for the sum of a product infinity and an
infinity of the other sign; an infinity if either term is infinite; a zero with the
sign of both terms when both are zeros of one sign, and otherwise, for an exact
zero sum, $+0$, or $-0$ when \sig{rnd\_mode} is 2.

\section{Accuracy}

\sig{ffma} is correctly rounded: it passes the TestFloat \sig{f32\_mulAdd} vectors
in all five rounding modes (Section~\ref{sec:testfloat}), and the binary16 and
binary64 ones.
